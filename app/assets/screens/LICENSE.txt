The two screen photographs in this directory
============================================

    start.png        behind the title screen
    background.png   behind the table selector and the mission screen

⚠️ THEIR PROVENANCE IS NOT YET RECORDED, AND THAT IS A DECISION OWED RATHER THAN
AN OVERSIGHT.

The six playfield pictures in `app/assets/tables/` are dedicated under CC0 1.0
on a fact the Dev supplied about them — they are machine-generated, by Google
Gemini, prompted by him — and `docs/LICENSES.md` §4.1 records why that fact, and
not a preference, decided the licence. A Creative Commons licence operates on a
copyright; a copyright needs a human author (Lei 9.610/98 art. 11); so what a
picture may be licensed as depends on who or what made it.

These two arrived as `art/start.jpg` and `art/background.jpg` with no such fact
attached. Writing CC0 on them by analogy would be asserting the same conclusion
from an assumption instead of from evidence, in the one file whose whole purpose
is to state obligations accurately — which is exactly what §4.1 says this
repository does not get to do.

So this note records what IS known:

    derived by    reduction to 320x180, dimming to a ceiling of Y 0.1469 (the
                  contrast the screens' text needs against them), and
                  quantisation to 192 colours, by scripts/import-art.py
    masters       art/start.jpg and art/background.jpg, not versioned — see
                  .gitignore and art/README.md
    supplied by   José Rocha, September 2026
    made by       NOT RECORDED

What to write here once that is answered
----------------------------------------

If they were generated the way the playfields were, this file becomes a copy of
`app/assets/tables/LICENSE.txt` with the two names changed, and `art/README.md`
and `docs/LICENSES.md` §4.1 gain a line each.

If a person made them, or if they came from somewhere else, then the answer is
whatever that person or that source licenses them under, and pillar 10 applies
in its ordinary form: the art is not ours to licence, and it travels beside the
code under its own terms rather than under the AGPL.

The code that draws them is AGPL-3.0-or-later like the rest of the repository.
