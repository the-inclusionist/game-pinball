Why CC0 and not a share-alike licence
-------------------------------------

The first choice was CC BY-SA 4.0, to match the reason the code is AGPL: work
that stays open when someone reuses it. It was changed on one fact — the
pictures are machine-generated.

A Creative Commons licence operates ON A COPYRIGHT. It grants permissions a
rights-holder has and attaches conditions to them; where there is no copyright
there is nothing for it to act on. A copyright needs a human author: Lei
9.610/98 art. 11, "Autor é a pessoa física criadora de obra literária, artística
ou científica", and the United States Copyright Office has reached the same
conclusion for material without human authorship.

So ShareAlike here would assert a restriction nobody would have standing to
enforce. CC0 works whether or not a copyright exists: it dedicates any rights
that may exist, and carries a permissive fallback licence for the jurisdictions
where a dedication is not possible. It asserts nothing that might turn out to be
false.

Using the output was never the same question. Google's terms for Gemini do not
claim ownership of output and permit its use. That is PERMISSION, and only the
copyright question decides what licence can be applied.

The full argument, and what would change the answer, is in docs/LICENSES.md §4.1.

Note that this dedication covers THESE FILES. The code that draws them is
AGPL-3.0-or-later like the rest of the repository; the two travel together as an
aggregate, each under its own terms.
